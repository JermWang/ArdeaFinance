BETA Arm a wallet · routed through Monero

Arm once. Fire whenever.

Private routes through Monero take 10–40 minutes, too slow for a coin that's moving. So Ardea does the slow part first. You arm one to five clean wallets; each is paid through Monero by an exchange, not by you. When you want in, the next armed wallet buys instantly.

Armed ahead

Arm one to five wallets in one approval. Each gets its own Monero route, sent at random intervals, at uneven sizes.

A real Monero hop

Every route is forced through Monero, where ring signatures, stealth addresses and RingCT hide sender, receiver and amount. Houdini Swap runs the route; Ardea never holds your funds or XMR.

Fires instantly

With Private mode on, any buy or ⚡ quick-buy fires the oldest armed wallet with its whole balance, signed in your tab. No popups, no wait.

Out of step

The funding happened earlier and in different amounts, so the buy's timing and size don't line up with your deposit, the match trackers look for.

How arming works

  1. Arm. Pick a total and how many wallets. For each, the terminal opens a SOL → XMR → SOL route with Houdini Swap, forced through Monero, and checks the order: right wallet, right amount, a private route, or it refuses.
  2. Approve once. One wallet prompt covers every funding transfer: plain transfers to one-time addresses, nothing else. Ardea's 0.5% fee is taken inside the route. They go out at random intervals. You see what will arrive before you sign.
  3. It crosses Monero. One exchange swaps the SOL to XMR, the XMR moves on Monero, and an unrelated exchange pays each wallet from its own funds, in 10–40 minutes, in the background. If no Monero route is available, nothing is sent.
  4. Fire. Any buy fires the next armed wallet through Jupiter, instantly. Sell from it whenever; disarming returns its SOL to you through Monero.

No armed wallet ready? Private mode still works: that buy waits for its own route.

What it doesn't hide. Houdini Swap sees both ends of each route (it keeps order data for 72 hours), and each exchange sees its own leg. A determined observer can still try to match amounts and timing. Arming ahead, at uneven sizes and random intervals, breaks the simple match between your deposit and your buy; it can't make tracing impossible. An armed wallet's key lives in your browser, derived from your signature, so keep only what you mean to trade in it. See the proof.
