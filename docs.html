Overview

A trading desk that leaves fewer tracks.

Ardea is a Solana trading terminal that runs entirely in your browser. It does what fast terminals do: a live board of new pump.fun launches, one-click buys, token pages with live trades and holders, wallet tracking, limit orders and PnL. On top of that it gives you two things the others don't: Private mode, which buys from fresh wallets funded through Monero, and the Trail audit, which shows what wallet trackers can already link to you.

Non-custodial

You sign every trade, in your wallet or in wallets derived from your own signature. Ardea never holds funds.

Best-price routing

Every swap is routed by Jupiter across Solana's DEXs and pump.fun curves.

Private funding

Stealth wallets are paid by an exchange after a Monero hop, not by you, so no transfer runs from you to them.

Static and open

The app is plain files: HTML and JavaScript you can read, host or run locally.

Wallets

One signature, every wallet.

You connect a normal Solana wallet (Phantom, Solflare, Backpack and others). That is your main wallet. When you first use the instant wallet or Private mode, the terminal asks it to sign one fixed message. The signature never leaves your browser and is never stored. From it, the terminal derives:

WalletSigns withUse it for
MainYour wallet app, with a popup per tradeHolding funds, funding the others, careful trades
InstantA key in this tab's memory, no popups quick buys and presets when you trade from it, plus autopilot and snipes. Fund it directly or privately.
StealthA fresh key per private buyBuys with no direct transfer from your main wallet

Recovery

Solana signatures are deterministic: the same wallet signing the same message always produces the same signature. Sign again on any device and you get the same instant and stealth wallets back. Nothing secret needs to be backed up beyond your main wallet.

The terminal stores a short fingerprint of the signature (8 bytes of its hash, which reveals nothing). If your wallet ever signs differently, for example a different wallet app that signs messages another way, the terminal stops instead of opening a different set of wallets.

Every derived wallet has an Export key button, so you can import it into any Solana wallet.

Only sign the Ardea message on Ardea. Anyone holding that signature can rebuild your instant and stealth wallets; the message itself warns that it opens your private trading wallets. Some hardware-wallet setups can't sign messages; with those, instant and Private mode are unavailable and you trade from the main wallet.

Trading

From click to confirmed.

  1. The terminal asks Jupiter for a quote: your amount, slippage from the active preset, routes limited so the transaction has room for the fee.
  2. It fetches Jupiter's swap instructions, not a finished transaction, and assembles the transaction itself: compute budget, the fee transfer, the swap, cleanup.
  3. You sign. Main-wallet trades open a popup; instant and stealth trades sign in the tab.
  4. It sends the transaction, rebroadcasts it every 2 seconds until it lands or its blockhash expires, and reports the result.

Jito and MEV-protect

With a tip set, the transaction pays it to one of Jito's tip accounts and is also sent to Jito's block engine, which helps it land in busy blocks. With MEV-protect on, the terminal simulates the transaction first and sends it only to Jito, never to a public RPC node, which keeps it away from sandwich bots watching the network.

Fees

0.5%, in SOL, in the open.

Every terminal trade pays a 0.5% fee in SOL, inside the same transaction as the swap. You see it in the trade panel and in your wallet's preview before you sign.

  • Buys: 0.5% of the SOL you spend, transferred before the swap.
  • Sells: 0.5% of the minimum SOL the route guarantees, transferred after the swap, so a fill better than the minimum is all yours.
  • Private buys and arming: 0.5%, taken inside the Monero route as Houdini partner markup (0.25% on each of its two legs) and paid to Ardea's Houdini partner account. Your funding is one plain transfer to a one-time address, with no transfer to Ardea in it, so nothing on-chain marks it as an Ardea route.
  • Not charged: stealth exits, deposits to and withdrawals from your instant wallet, and limit orders (Jupiter fills those).

Network costs are separate and go to Solana validators: the base fee, the priority fee from your preset, and a Jito tip if set. Jupiter's routing is free.

Arm a wallet

Do the slow part first. Fire when it counts.

A private route through Monero takes 10–40 minutes, too slow for a coin that's moving. Arming moves that wait to before you need it. You fund one to five clean stealth wallets through Monero ahead of time. Each one is armed once its SOL lands, and with Private mode on, any buy or ⚡ quick-buy fires the oldest armed wallet instantly.

  1. Choose a total and a number of wallets (one to five, up to 5 SOL each in beta). The total is split into uneven, non-round sizes.
  2. For each wallet the terminal derives the next fresh stealth wallet, checks the chain that it has never been used, opens a Houdini private-send order forced through Monero, and checks the order exactly as a private buy does. Anything off and it refuses.
  3. You approve every funding transfer in one wallet prompt: each a plain transfer to its order's one-time deposit address, nothing else. The 0.5% fee is taken inside the routes. The terminal sends them 3–15 seconds apart, at random.
  4. Each route crosses Monero in 10–40 minutes, in the background, and resumes after a reload. When a wallet's SOL lands it's marked armed.
  5. Firing uses the oldest armed wallet: it swaps its whole balance (keeping 0.01 SOL for fees and token-account rent) through Jupiter, signed in your tab, with no fee. Each armed wallet fires once.
  6. Disarming sends a wallet's SOL, or what's left after selling, back to your wallet through the same Monero route.

Why it's more private, not just faster

The simplest way to unmask a private buy is to match it to its funding: SOL leaves your wallet, a similar amount reaches a fresh wallet, and that wallet buys soon after. Arming breaks that pattern. The funding happens earlier, at uneven sizes, at random intervals, and the buy happens whenever you fire, with whatever arrived after the route's fees.

What arming doesn't change. Houdini sees both ends of each route, and each exchange sees its own leg. An armed wallet's key lives in your browser, derived from your wallet's signature like every stealth wallet: anyone with that signature could rebuild it, so keep in armed wallets only what you mean to trade. Arming makes tracing slower and less certain; it doesn't make it impossible.

Private mode · through Monero

Monero pays your stealth wallet. You never do.

Trackers link wallets by following SOL: whoever funds a wallet is assumed to own it. Private mode breaks that link by never sending SOL from your wallet to the stealth wallet. Instead your SOL crosses Monero, whose ring signatures, stealth addresses and RingCT hide sender, receiver and amount on its own chain. Houdini Swap's private send runs the route: one exchange swaps your SOL to XMR, the XMR moves on Monero, and an unrelated exchange swaps it back to SOL and pays the stealth wallet from its own hot wallet. You never hold XMR or need a Monero wallet.

  1. The terminal derives the next stealth wallet from your signature and checks the chain that it has never been used, so a cleared browser or a new device never reuses one.
  2. It asks Houdini for a private-send quote with Monero forced as the bridge (useXmr=true), takes the best private route, and opens the order with the stealth wallet as receiver and your wallet for refunds. If no Monero route is available, nothing is sent; there is no other path.
  3. It checks the order before you sign: it must pay exactly the stealth wallet, swap SOL to SOL on Solana, be a private (anonymous) route, keep the amount, use a deposit address that isn't one of yours, need no memo, and not expire too soon. Otherwise it refuses.
  4. You approve one transaction: a plain transfer of the amount to the order's one-time deposit address, nothing else. The 0.5% fee is taken inside the route. The terminal shows the SOL that will arrive and the route time first.
  5. The route runs in the background, usually 10–40 minutes (exchange confirmations plus Monero's confirmation and lock time). The terminal stays usable, follows the order's status, and picks it back up if you reload.
  6. When the SOL lands, Strong mode (the default) waits a random 30 seconds to 3 minutes, then the stealth wallet buys on Jupiter, keeping 0.01 SOL for network fees and token-account rent. Fast mode buys on arrival.

Exiting

What it costs

The Ardea fee, Solana network fees, and the route's own cost: two exchange swaps, network fees and Houdini's commission, all inside the quote. It's roughly double a single swap, so the terminal shows exactly how much SOL arrives before you sign, and each route has a minimum amount. Private buys are capped at 5 SOL while Private mode is in beta.

If something goes wrong

A route that can't complete is refunded by the exchange to the wallet that sent it, and Houdini's support can trace any order by its id, which Portfolio keeps. Stealth wallets can always be rebuilt by signing again, and Portfolio scans your stealth wallets on-chain and lists every one with funds, with buttons to exit or export its key, so nothing is stranded. If you've made many stealth wallets, Scan further looks deeper.

What Private mode does not do. Nothing makes a wallet untraceable. Houdini sees both ends of a route and keeps order data for 72 hours; each exchange sees its own leg. An observer who sees SOL leave your wallet and a similar amount reach a fresh wallet later can guess; the route's fees change the amount and its 10–40 minutes plus Strong mode's random wait blur the timing, which makes that harder, not impossible. Vary amounts, and don't send funds between stealth wallets and wallets you've used publicly. Private mode makes tracing expensive and uncertain; it doesn't make it impossible. Ardea doesn't run a mixer and never touches the funds in transit.

Why Monero

Monero is the one widely used chain where sender, receiver and amount are hidden by default for everyone, so the hop in the middle isn't a public record a tracker can read. The trade-off is time and cost: two exchange hops and Monero's lock time make a route slower and pricier than a bridge. That's why the terminal funds a stealth wallet once and lets you keep trading while the route runs.

Trail audit

        
What trackers already know.

        
The Trail audit reads any wallet's public history and scores how linkable it is, 0 to 100, the way commercial trackers score it. It's read-only: nothing to connect or sign. Your browser sends its queries through Ardea's gateway to the RPC node, or straight to your own node if you set one under RPC node.

FAQ

        
Questions.

        
Can Ardea take my funds?
No. It has no server holding funds or wallet keys, and its gateway only forwards requests: it never signs anything. Every transaction is built in your browser and signed by you or by a key derived from your own signature. Private funds in transit are held by the route's exchanges and refunded to you if a route fails.

        
Why did my trade fail?
Usually the price moved past your slippage, or the transaction didn't land before it expired. Nothing but the network fee is spent when a swap fails. Try a faster preset or more slippage.

        
Is Private mode anonymous?
No tool makes you anonymous. Private mode removes the direct on-chain link between your wallet and the wallet that buys. Timing and amounts can still be matched by someone watching closely, so vary them.

        
Where are my instant and stealth wallets if I clear my browser?
Their keys were never stored, so nothing is lost. Connect the same main wallet, sign the same message, and they come back with their balances. Clearing the browser also deletes the local list linking your stealth wallets to your main wallet; the terminal finds them again on-chain.

        
Do limit orders pay the 0.5% fee?
Not today. Jupiter fills limit orders itself; terminal market trades and private buys pay the fee.

        
Why is a sniper or stop loss tied to the open tab?
Because it signs from the instant wallet, whose key only exists in your tab. Nothing on a server can sign for you. For orders that must fill while you're away, use a limit order.
